History

From an idea in 1973 to breaking the 10‑hour barrier. Based on the account by Marian Kula.

  1. 1973

    Marian Kula, finishing his C‑class catamaran “Mata Hari”, comes up with the idea of a timed attempt over the 200‑mile route along the Polish coast. One principle from the British speed‑record rules makes it into the regulations and still applies today: crew muscle power only.

  2. 1982

    Thanks to Tadeusz Ejchstaedt, the foreign‑trade company PHZ Navimor and the weekly “Wybrzeże” become patrons. Navimor funds a silver challenge trophy.

  3. 1983

    The event starts as the “Sailing Route Record – Navimor‑Wybrzeże Cup”. On 2 September “Hetman”, skippered by Jan Pinkiewicz, sets the baseline time: 41 h 17 min. Later that month “Wojewoda Pomorski” cuts it to 30 h 35 min.

  4. 1984

    The year of greatest interest and the most records broken. Opal‑class yachts get a separate classification and their own crystal trophy. Wojciech Kaliski’s catamaran “Almatur III” clocks 19 h 42 min — a result that stands for 15 years.

  5. 1985

    After three seasons: more than 30 crews and the best time improved seven times. “Komandor II” sets the Opal‑class record that still stands. Windsurfer Wojciech Tworzydło covers the route in 7 days, outside the classification — the rules admit craft from 6.5 m.

  6. 1987

    After the Polish Championships in Świnoujście, five yachts race for the record on their way to Gdynia, without spinnakers. “Hadar”, fastest across the line, and runner‑up “Kapitan II” are disqualified after a protest over additional genoas; the record goes to “Kneź” — 23 h 12 min.

  7. 1999

    Roman Paszke on the catamaran “Alka Prim” beats the time of “Almatur III”: 13 h 50 min.

  8. 2003

    The Committee changes the rules to admit single‑handed sailors. Zbigniew Szafranowicz on “Morka” becomes the first solo record holder (28 h 06 min). “Łódka Bols” sets the monohull record: 16 h.

  9. 2007

    The Exploder 25 trimaran “Insgraf” with Wojciech Kaliski’s three‑man crew: 13 h 32 min.

  10. 2009

    Two attempts by Roman Paszke’s “Gemini 3”: 13 h 07 min in August, and on 25 October the first time ever under 10 hours: 8 h 55 min.

How to beat the record?

Roman Paszke’s recipe, from an interview with Krzysztof Olejnik (“Wiatr” magazine): a fast yacht, a well‑drilled crew and a weather window with SW or NW wind of about 30 knots. The attempt must be notified to the Committee a few days before the start; the Committee supervises the timing.

Marian Kula believes “Gemini 3” is capable of covering the route in as little as five hours.

The rules in brief

Key rules of the Sailing Route Record. Only the full Polish text of the regulations is binding.

1

Who can take part

Sailing sports craft from 6.5 m in length, with no limits on class, type or sail area. Each attempt is made individually, at a time chosen by the competitor.

2

Season and notification

Attempts from 1 May to 31 October. Notify the Committee by e‑mail or phone, giving the skipper, yacht name, sail number and the day and hour of the start.

3

Route

Gdynia – Świnoujście or Świnoujście – Gdynia, about 200 NM. The start and finish lines are set at the port entrance heads (details next to the map).

4

Wind and muscle only

Sail propulsion only; the Committee may require the engine to be sealed. Gear is operated by crew muscle power. Other energy sources only for lighting, cooking and navigation electronics.

5

Timing

Start and finish times are entered in the logbook; the result is calculated to the nearest minute. A new record must beat the previous one by at least one minute.

6

Ratification

The result is submitted in writing (voyage card, logbook extract). The Route Record Committee ratifies the record and confirms it with a certificate. The notified skipper must be on board throughout the attempt.

The owner and the skipper alone are responsible for the safety of the yacht and crew and for the decision to start and to continue the attempt.
