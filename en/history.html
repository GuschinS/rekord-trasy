History

From an idea to the Route Record

Świnoujście – Gdynia – Świnoujście

Based on the account by Marian Kula

Click a chapter title to read it in full.

From the idea to the first rulesWhere the idea of a 200‑mile time trial along the Polish coast came from and how the rules were written.More

The idea was born in 1973, as Marian Kula was finishing his own international C‑class catamaran “Mata Hari”. The boat had been designed back in 1970 by Gdynia designer Norbert Patalas; it was the first catamaran in Poland with a wing mast, built by the Experimental Glider Works in Bielsko. Kazimierz Maik built the hulls somewhat stronger and heavier than the class required, which suggested testing the boat on the 200‑mile course along the coast, in either direction.

Kula wrote to the English World Sailing Speed Record Committee, administered by the Royal Yachting Association, asking for rules on which to base his own. He received a two‑page document, but it only covered the speed record over a 500 m run. He carried over just one principle into the Polish rules: gear on board is worked by crew muscle power only, while mechanical and hydraulic transmissions are allowed. That sentence, which its author never changed, appears in every later version, and the rules remain almost unchanged to this day.

The idea only came to life in 1982, when Tadeusz Ejchstaedt — a sailor working in advertising at Navimor — joined the crew of “Admirał” (formerly “Kneź”, later “Nauticus” and “Hadar”). He became the driving force: he persuaded PHZ Navimor to act as patron and brought in the weekly “Wybrzeże”, whose editor‑in‑chief Jerzy Ringer assigned journalist Henryk Spigarski to the project. Journalist Zenon Gralak and Norbert Patalas joined too.

Navimor funded an impressive silver challenge trophy — a chalice the workshop had made in one extra copy for the Pope’s visit to Poland and decorated with the record’s emblem. The Route Record logo was designed by Janusz Wesołowski, graphic artist of “Biznesman” magazine. Wojciech Kaliski won the trophy outright after two years; when it was later to be borrowed as a model for a new one, it turned out to be lost, and its fate is still unknown.

1983The “Sailing Route Record – Navimor‑Wybrzeże Cup” begins: “Hetman” sets the baseline time and the record falls for the first time.More

The event started as the “Sailing Route Record – Navimor‑Wybrzeże Cup”. The beginnings were not easy: the author’s home club, “Kotwica”, at first declined to co‑organise it, so Marian Kula turned to skipper Zbigniew Szpetulski, commodore of AKM at the Gdynia Maritime School, his regatta rival — Szpetulski on “Wojewoda Pomorski”, Kula on “Admirał”. Szpetulski agreed at once.

When the press credited AKM with the whole organisation, the author decided to bring the record back where it belonged. Still in 1983 the Route Record returned to JKMW “Kotwica”, which agreed that Marian Kula would keep the entries and times and that the club rooms would host the trophy presentation.

The author persuaded skipper Jan Pinkiewicz of “Kotwica” to set the baseline time for others to beat. On 2 September 1983 Pinkiewicz sailed from Świnoujście to Gdynia on the club yacht “Hetman” with a crew of nine in 41 hours 17 minutes, becoming the first record holder. He did not enjoy it for long: later that September skipper Janusz Nadolny‑Zawadowicz of AKM Gdańsk, on “Wojewoda Pomorski” with a crew of ten, clocked 30 hours 35 minutes. Tough competition started from that result.

1984The year of greatest interest — the most entries, attempts and records broken, and a separate Opal‑class classification.More

In 1984 the Committee received the most entries and the most attempts were made; no other year saw so many records broken.

The Opal Class Yacht Association proposed classifying and rewarding these yachts separately from that season — with competition getting tougher, cruising yachts could not keep up with pure racing yachts. The Committee agreed, changed the rules and funded an extra crystal trophy. From then on the fight was for two records and two challenge trophies, each becoming the property of a skipper whose record stands for two years.

Three records fell in the Opal class. The last, still unbeaten record holder is “Komandor II” of “Kotwica”; skipper Kazimierz Klugman held the record for two years and received the crystal trophy for good from the hands of “Wybrzeże” editor‑in‑chief Jerzy Ringer. The same year Wojciech Kaliski’s catamaran “Almatur III” clocked 19 hours 42 minutes.

1985After three seasons — over 30 crews and seven record improvements; the silver trophy goes to Wojciech Kaliski.More

When the idea was announced in 1982, many sailors were sceptical — doubting anyone would bother with such a game or that a silver trophy would attract crews. It soon turned out that after the start of “Hetman” more and more yachts came forward to show fast sailing. In January 1986 journalist Zenon Gralak wrote in “Głos Wybrzeża” that the event had won full recognition among sailors and livened up offshore racing.

Within three seasons more than 30 crews attempted the record, and the best time from Świnoujście to Gdynia was improved seven times — that direction, with the wind from west to east, is the best for record sailing.

The record attracted attention far and wide: Kraków windsurfer Wojciech Tworzydło sailed from Świnoujście to Gdynia on 14–21 July 1985 in 7 days 1 hour 40 minutes. He could not be classified, as the rules did not provide for sailboards — for safety the smallest craft admitted are 6.5 m long, modelled on the seaworthy Mini‑Transat yachts.

Wojciech Kaliski was in a class of his own. The record of his catamaran “Almatur III” stood for two years, so Navimor’s silver trophy became his. The time of 19 hours 42 minutes proved so good that it was only beaten 15 years later by Roman Paszke on the catamaran “Alka Prim” — 13 hours 50 minutes in 1999.

1987A race for the Route Record after the Polish Championships, a protest and the record of “Kneź”.More

After the Polish Championships in Świnoujście, several skippers agreed to race for the Route Record on the way back to Gdynia. “Kneź” (skipper Władysław Beyer, YKP Gdynia) started together with “Kapitan II” (skipper Edward Kinas), “Nauticus” (skipper Krzysztof Matla), “Hadar” (skipper Marian Kula) and “Tornado” (skipper Tadeusz Siwiec) of “Kotwica”. The tired crews agreed not to use spinnakers.

The wind was NW up to force 7. When it veered to a dead run the yachts were hard to keep on course, and the 80 m² genoas kept wrapping around the forestays. “Kapitan II”, “Kneź” and “Hadar” sailed close together for a long time, while the fastest boat, “Tornado”, inexplicably fell behind. To keep steerage, “Kapitan II” and “Hadar” set extra genoas in the second luff groove of the same forestay — about 200 m² of sail with the main. “Kneź” had a single‑groove forestay foil and could not do the same.

“Hadar” finished first, “Kapitan II” 15 minutes later and “Kneź” 20 minutes after that. Skipper Beyer protested against the extra genoas; skipper Kinas and Marian Kula agreed, judging that the “Kneź” crew — slower, yet in the lead for much of the race — deserved the record. “Kapitan II” and “Hadar” were disqualified, the 23 hours 12 minutes record went to “Kneź”, “Nauticus” was second and “Tornado” third.

“Hadar” tried a few more times in the “wrong” direction, east to west — once beating every record as far as Ustka, but beyond Ustka the wind died and she had to motor to Świnoujście. No attempts followed for years; activity only returned in 2003.

2003The first single‑handed sailor and a change of rules; the solo record of “Morka”.More

In 2003 skipper Zbigniew Szafranowicz applied to start as the first single‑hander on his own monohull “Morka”. His determination and sensible approach to solo sailing convinced the Committee, which changed the rules at its meeting to admit a well and safely prepared solo sailor.

Szafranowicz became the first record holder in the single‑handed category with 28 hours 6 minutes. The same year “Łódka Bols”, skippered by Jarosław Kaczorowski with a crew of seventeen, set the monohull record of 16 hours. No attempts were recorded for the next few years, until in 2007 Wojciech Kaliski clocked 13 hours 32 minutes on the Exploder 25 trimaran “Insgraf”.

2009Two attempts by the catamaran “Gemini 3” and the first time under 10 hours.More

In August and October 2009 the Committee accepted two entries from Roman Paszke on the maxi catamaran “Gemini 3”. Everyone had long waited for the 10‑hour barrier to fall, wondering who would do it and when.

In the first attempt, in August, the “Gemini 3” crew narrowly beat the record of the trimaran “Insgraf” — a boat less than half its length. In the second, on 25 October 2009, they beat their own August time by more than four hours, with 8 hours 55 minutes. It is a hard record to beat — according to Marian Kula, probably only by a foreign multihull such as “Banque Populaire”, regarded as the fastest in the world.

Twenty‑seven years after it began, the Route Record has become a permanent part of Polish sailing, although it often met with hostility and misunderstanding, and sometimes years passed before the next record.

From the start the author knew the time would come for modern yachts built specifically for high speed — like Wojciech Kaliski’s trimaran and catamaran or Roman Paszke’s catamarans. The event was designed for them and for enthusiasts like them. Records are not regattas and do not happen on demand — that is the magic of the Route Record.

2026Live tracking of attempts and the new Double‑handed category.More

From 1 September 2026 every attempt on the 200 NM route is monitored in real time with the OMNI REGATTA RACE RECORDER app, whose data is the basis for confirming the time.

On 19–20 October 2026 Roman Paszke and Aleksander Pieciukiewicz on the yacht “Lara Jane” (POL44) sailed from Gdynia to Świnoujście in 27:00:54 — 206.26 NM at an average of 7.6 knots and a top speed of 16.0 knots — setting a reference time in the new Double‑handed category.
