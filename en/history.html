History

From an idea to the Route Record

Świnoujście – Gdynia – Świnoujście

Based on the account by Marian Kula

Click a year to read more.

1973Marian Kula, finishing his C‑class catamaran “Mata Hari”, comes up with the idea of a timed attempt over the 200‑mile route along the Polish coast. One principle from the British speed‑record rules makes it into the regulations and still applies today: crew muscle power only.More

The idea came to Marian Kula as he was finishing his own international C‑class catamaran “Mata Hari”, designed in 1970 by Gdynia designer Norbert Patalas — the first catamaran in Poland with a wing mast, built by the Experimental Glider Works in Bielsko. Kazimierz Maik built the hulls somewhat stronger and heavier than usual. Kula wanted to test the boat on the 200‑mile course along the coast, in either direction. He asked the British world sailing speed record committee (administered by the RYA) for its rules, but they only covered a 500 m run. One principle made it into the Polish regulations and still applies: all gear is worked by crew muscle power only.

1982Thanks to Tadeusz Ejchstaedt, the foreign‑trade company PHZ Navimor and the weekly “Wybrzeże” become patrons. Navimor funds a silver challenge trophy.More

The breakthrough came when Tadeusz Ejchstaedt, a sailor working in Navimor’s advertising department, joined the crew of “Admirał” (ex “Kneź”). He persuaded the foreign‑trade company PHZ Navimor to become patron and brought in the weekly “Wybrzeże” and its editor‑in‑chief Jerzy Ringer; journalists Henryk Spigarski and Zenon Gralak and designer Norbert Patalas also supported the event. Navimor funded a silver challenge trophy, and the record’s logo was designed by Janusz Wesołowski. The trophy, later won outright by Wojciech Kaliski, was eventually lost and its fate is still unknown.

1983The event starts as the “Sailing Route Record – Navimor‑Wybrzeże Cup”. On 2 September “Hetman”, skippered by Jan Pinkiewicz, sets the baseline time: 41 h 17 min. Later that month “Wojewoda Pomorski” cuts it to 30 h 35 min.More

At first the event was run by the Academic Maritime Club of the Gdynia Maritime School, but within the same year the Route Record returned to JKMW “Kotwica”, where Marian Kula kept the entries and timing records. On 2 September skipper Jan Pinkiewicz set the baseline time on the club yacht “Hetman” with a crew of nine: 41 h 17 min from Świnoujście to Gdynia. Later that month skipper Janusz Nadolny‑Zawadowicz of AKM Gdańsk cut it to 30 h 35 min on “Wojewoda Pomorski” — and real competition began.

1984The year of greatest interest and the most records broken. Opal‑class yachts get a separate classification and their own crystal trophy. Wojciech Kaliski’s catamaran “Almatur III” clocks 19 h 42 min — a result that stands for 15 years.More

The year with the most entries, attempts and records broken. At the request of the Opal Class Yacht Association the Committee started to classify these yachts separately — fearing they could not compete with pure racing yachts — and funded a crystal challenge trophy for them. A trophy becomes the property of a skipper whose record stands for two years. Three records fell in the Opal class. Wojciech Kaliski’s catamaran “Almatur III” clocked 19 h 42 min.

1985After three seasons: more than 30 crews and the best time improved seven times. “Komandor II” sets the Opal‑class record that still stands. Windsurfer Wojciech Tworzydło covers the route in 7 days, outside the classification — the rules admit craft from 6.5 m.More

Although many sailors were sceptical at first, more than 30 crews made attempts within three seasons and the best time was improved seven times — mostly in the more favourable west‑to‑east direction. The Opal‑class record was set by “Komandor II” of “Kotwica”; skipper Kazimierz Klugman held it for two years and kept the crystal trophy. Wojciech Kaliski kept Navimor’s silver trophy for good. Kraków windsurfer Wojciech Tworzydło covered the route in 7 days 1 h 40 min, but a sailboard was outside the rules, which for safety admit craft from 6.5 m — following the Mini‑Transat class.

1987After the Polish Championships in Świnoujście, five yachts race for the record on their way to Gdynia, without spinnakers. “Hadar”, fastest across the line, and runner‑up “Kapitan II” are disqualified after a protest over additional genoas; the record goes to “Kneź” — 23 h 12 min.More

After the Polish Championships in Świnoujście the skippers decided to sail back to Gdynia as a race for the Route Record: “Kneź” (skipper Władysław Beyer, YKP Gdynia) started together with “Kapitan II”, “Nauticus”, “Hadar” and “Tornado” of “Kotwica”. The tired crews agreed to sail without spinnakers; the wind was NW up to force 7. “Kapitan II” and “Hadar” set two genoas on one forestay — about 200 m² of sail with the main. “Hadar” finished first, but after a protest by skipper Beyer both yachts were disqualified and the 23 h 12 min record went to “Kneź”. No attempts followed for many years.

1999Roman Paszke on the catamaran “Alka Prim” beats the time of “Almatur III”: 13 h 50 min.More

The multihull record of “Almatur III”, which had stood for 15 years, was beaten by Roman Paszke on the F‑40 catamaran “Alka Prim” with a crew of five (Polfarma Starogard Gdański): 13 h 50 min.

2003The Committee changes the rules to admit single‑handed sailors. Zbigniew Szafranowicz on “Morka” becomes the first solo record holder (28 h 06 min). “Łódka Bols” sets the monohull record: 16 h.More

The first single‑hander applied — skipper Zbigniew Szafranowicz on his own monohull “Morka”. His determination and sensible preparation persuaded the Committee to change the rules and admit well‑prepared solo sailors. He clocked 28 h 06 min. The same year “Łódka Bols”, skippered by Jarosław Kaczorowski with a crew of seventeen, set the monohull record: 16 h.

2007The Exploder 25 trimaran “Insgraf” with Wojciech Kaliski’s three‑man crew: 13 h 32 min.More

Wojciech Kaliski returned to the route with the Exploder 25 trimaran “Insgraf” and a crew of three, improving on “Alka Prim” with 13 h 32 min.

2009Two attempts by Roman Paszke’s “Gemini 3”: 13 h 07 min in August, and on 25 October the first time ever under 10 hours: 8 h 55 min.More

Roman Paszke entered the maxi catamaran “Gemini 3” (formerly “Bioton”) twice. In August the crew narrowly beat the time of “Insgraf”, a boat less than half its length — 13 h 07 min. On 25 October, with an offshore wind of up to 27 knots, they covered the route in 8 h 55 min, breaking the 10‑hour barrier for the first time. Marian Kula believes only a foreign multihull, such as “Banque Populaire”, could now beat this result.

2026From 1 September every attempt on the route is tracked live by OMNI REGATTA. On 19–20 October Roman Paszke and Aleksander Pieciukiewicz on “Lara Jane” set a reference time in the new Double‑handed category: 27 h 00 min 54 s (Gdynia → Świnoujście).More

From 1 September every attempt is tracked live with the OMNI REGATTA RACE RECORDER app, whose data is the basis for confirming the time. On 19–20 October Roman Paszke and Aleksander Pieciukiewicz on the yacht “Lara Jane” (POL44) sailed from Gdynia to Świnoujście in 27:00:54 — 206.26 NM at an average of 7.6 knots — setting a reference time in the new Double‑handed category.
